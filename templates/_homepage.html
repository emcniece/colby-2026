Platform

The future is bright

My aim is to make Langford more affordable, connected, and fun. I'm focusing on bringing the right people and ideas together to keep us moving forward, together.

  • Livable neighbourhoods

    Partner with non-profit housing providers to deliver homes working people can actually afford and create vibrant spaces with free community events so you can connect, play and stay active without breaking the bank.

FAQs

Your questions, answered

I've heard your questions while knocking on doors and talking at community events. I'm committed to being open about our challenges and transparent about my plan to solve them.

As a Langford taxpayer, I feel the pain of the increase too. Many of us are struggling to build a future in this city, and I don't take that lightly.

When we took office, we found that our city had been under-funding essential services like the fire department for years to keep taxes artificially low. We were essentially ignoring our city's real needs to keep the numbers looking good on paper. We were also using developer contributions to balance out ongoing operating expenses… rather than putting that money towards real public infrastructure. We had to make a choice: continue that 30-year pattern, or make the necessary investments to get our city on the right track.

Four years later, some of the tangible benefits include:

  • 15 new RCMP officers
  • 27 new full-time firefighters
  • A new medical clinic with 10 new family doctors
  • Safe sidewalks to schools

My goal is to ensure your tax dollars are being spent wisely to pay for the services we need today, rather than just covering up long-term problems.

We've already made significant investments — adding 27 new full-time firefighters, 15 RCMP officers, strengthening bylaw enforcement, and by supporting the new Westshore RCMP detachment. Going forward, my focus is to continue supporting front-line resources while being strategic and proactive on community safety with safer streets, expanded youth mental health services, and emergency services that keep pace with our growing population.

Planning for the future is always a good investment and consultant spending is sometimes necessary and responsible. Hiring independent experts to help create city plans ensures that Langford gets unbiased, technical advice on complex infrastructure decisions but it doesn’t mean local voices get shut out of the process. While there’s been an upfront investment, having complete plans in place have also proven to be a key component for the City to secure grant funding.

Change in local government is often slower and less visible than people expect — and that's okay. It means new medical clinics, updated zoning rules that allow more housing types, a Transportation Master Plan that didn't exist before, and a fire department that's finally properly staffed. I'm proud of that work, and there's much more still to do.

Local elections are decided by small margins — sometimes just a few hundred votes. The councillors elected in October will make decisions about your taxes, your roads, your parks, and the housing in your neighbourhood for the next four years. Every vote directly shapes the city you live in.
