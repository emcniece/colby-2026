FAQs

Your questions, answered

I've heard your questions while knocking on doors and talking at community events. I'm committed to being open about our challenges and transparent about my plan to solve them.

As a Langford taxpayer, I feel the pain of the increase too. Many of us are struggling to build a future in this city, and I don't take that lightly.

When we took office, we found that our city had been under-funding essential services like the fire department for years to keep taxes artificially low. We were essentially ignoring our city's real needs to keep the numbers looking good on paper. We had to make a choice: continue that pattern, or make the necessary investments to get our city on the right track.

We chose to catch up. Because of that investment, some of the tangible benefits are:

  • 27 new full-time firefighters
  • A new medical clinic with 12 new family doctors
  • Safe sidewalks to schools

My goal is to ensure your tax dollars are actually paying for the services we need, rather than just covering up long-term problems.

We've already made significant investments — hiring 27 new full-time firefighters and supporting the new Westshore RCMP detachment. Going forward, my focus is on proactive community safety: better street lighting, expanded mental health response options, and ensuring our emergency services keep pace with our growing population.
Some consultant spending is necessary and responsible — for example, hiring independent experts to develop our Transportation Master Plan ensures the city gets unbiased, technical advice on complex infrastructure decisions. Where I've pushed back is on consultants hired to do work that should be done by qualified city staff. I'm committed to transparency about what we spend and why.
Change in local government is often slower and less visible than people expect — and that's okay. It means new medical clinics, updated zoning rules that allow more housing types, a Transportation Master Plan that didn't exist before, and a fire department that's finally properly staffed. I'm proud of that work, and there's much more still to do.
Local elections are decided by small margins — sometimes just a few hundred votes. The councillors elected in October will make decisions about your taxes, your roads, your parks, and the housing in your neighbourhood for the next four years. Every vote directly shapes the city you live in.
